How It Works
  • Projection (cyan) follows the three steps in the 2006–2026 fWAR aging-curve write-up: (1) the year-1 projection is his full-season rate; (2) age the rate with the group's smoothed curve, the same number of wins for every player (starters per 180 IP, hitters per 600 PA); (3) multiply by playing-time retention, the share of base-age playing time established players still delivered k years later, counting a season out of the majors as zero. Retention uses the nearest base-age row (odd ages average the two neighbours) and is extended past eight years at its last rate of decline.
  • Ranges: 20,000 simulated careers around that path: projection error at signing (SD 0.6), talent drift (SD 0.5/yr), aging-curve uncertainty from the curve's 90% bootstrap interval, leaving the majors (about 70% of the retention drop, sooner for players who come in below projection), injury-shortened seasons, and season noise. Bad seasons are floored at −0.5, which lifts the mean about 0.05 fWAR a season above the write-up's table.
  • Contract-implied fWAR (amber): the year-1 projection the 2024–26 free-agent market would pay exactly this deal for, aged −0.4/yr as in the price fit. Price per win: $2.47M + $3.33M × min(projected fWAR, 3) above the minimum, in 2025 payroll dollars; ×1.065 for 2027, +3%/yr after; 5% discount; AAV paid evenly; minimum ~$0.8M rising 2.5%/yr.
  • Chance of positive value: share of simulated careers whose discounted fWAR beats the contract's discounted implied fWAR. Green above 75%, red below 25%. Fair AAV and expected surplus price the projected path at the same curve.
  • Caveats from the write-up: the fixed-win curve probably runs slightly optimistic for stars; retention comes from 1990–2017 cohorts, when starters threw more innings; retention partly overlaps the rate decline. Relievers aren't offered (no reliever contracts in the price fit). Signing bonuses, deferrals and opt-outs aren't modelled. The first season is 2027.
